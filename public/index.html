    About this app

    How to play

    Objective

    You command one ant colony living in a large, continuous world shared with other players. Send workers out to gather sugar, biomass, and protein, and to hunt aphids. Carry what they find back to your queen, and spend protein to hatch new workers — there's no fixed win condition, the goal is to grow your colony.

    Controls

    • Left-click a worker — select just that worker.
    • Left-drag on empty ground — box-select every worker inside the box.
    • Left-click empty ground (with workers selected) — send them there.
    • Left-click a resource node (sugar/protein/biomass, with workers selected) — they walk over, claim some, and automatically carry it home and deposit it.
    • Left-click an aphid (with workers selected) — they chase it down and attack until it dies, leaving behind a protein carcass you then gather like any other node.
    • Middle-drag or right-drag — pan the camera.
    • WASD or arrow keys — pan the camera continuously.
    • A worker already carrying resources home ignores new move/gather/attack orders until it deposits — reissue the command once it's free again.
    • Reproduce buttons — spend protein to hatch a new Worker, Soldier, or Scout; each button shows its current live cost.
    • Multiplayer — every browser session controls its own colony in one shared world. Other colonies' queens and workers are visible, moving in real time, but you can never select or command them, and your resources are private to you.
    • There is no player-vs-player combat. You cannot attack another player's queen or workers, and nothing you do can take or destroy another colony's resources. The only threat to your colony is a spider.

    Danger

    • Spiders are hostile. Unlike aphids, a spider actively hunts — it detects a nearby worker on its own, chases it down, and attacks on sight. Your workers never fight back automatically.
    • A worker being chased or attacked can still be sent to flee — move it away, and a spider that loses the chase eventually gives up and goes back to wandering.
    • To fight a spider instead, select worker(s) and left-click it, same as hunting an aphid. More workers attacking at once kill it faster.
    • Worker injury and death are real and permanent: a damaged worker shows a red health bar, and if its health reaches zero it dies and is removed from your colony for good — there's no revive.
    • A worker already carrying resources home keeps returning even while under attack — it won't automatically fight back or flee, so pull it out manually if it's in danger.
    • A killed spider leaves behind a protein carcass, just like an aphid — gather it explicitly like any other resource node.

    Colony Survival

    • Your queen can be killed. A spider that wanders or chases a fleeing worker close enough to your queen will attack her too — a red health bar appears above her once she's hurt, and she slowly regenerates health after a while undisturbed.
    • If your queen dies, your colony falls. All of your workers are removed and your stored resources are destroyed. Your old colony remains in the shared world as a non-interactive ruin — it is never deleted, looted, or rebuilt.
    • You can also abandon your colony voluntarily at any time via the "Abandon Colony" button — useful if you're stuck with no workers and not enough resources to recover. It has exactly the same effect as the queen being killed.

    Restarting

    • Once your colony has fallen (by death or abandonment), a "Start New Colony" button lets you begin again immediately, in the same shared world and the same browser session — no reload, no new link.
    • Your new colony spawns fresh, with full health and the standard starting workers and resources, somewhere else in the world, clear of other colonies, ruins, and spiders.
    • Starting a new colony never resets the shared world — every other colony, resource, and creature carries on exactly as it was.

    Evolution

    • As your colony reaches 5, 10, and 20 peak population (the highest worker count it's ever reached, not your current count), a new evolution tier unlocks — click the "Evolution" button to choose one of three permanent traits for that tier.
    • Each tier offers a Foraging, Survival, or Brood trait. Choices are freely mixable across tiers (no prerequisites) and permanent once made — there's no respec.
    • Foraging traits boost how much workers carry, how fast they gather, and how fast they move while carrying resources home. Survival traits boost worker health, attack damage, and combat speed. Brood traits make reproducing a new worker cheaper.
    • Evolution belongs to your colony, not your browser session — if your colony falls, its evolutionary history remains on the ruin forever, but a replacement colony via "Start New Colony" always begins completely fresh.

    Castes

    • Worker — the general-purpose caste: balanced health and speed, able to gather and carry resources, and the only caste that could reproduce before this release.
    • Soldier — a costly combat specialist with more health and attack damage but slower movement. A Soldier cannot gather or carry resources at all.
    • Scout — fast and fragile, with low health and a small carry capacity, but it sees much farther than any other unit, making it the best way to find distant resources and threats.
    • Evolution traits apply to all three castes — a Survival pick still boosts a Soldier's health and attack, a Foraging pick still boosts a Scout's gather/carry (where applicable), and every caste counts toward your colony's population and peak population.

    Fog of War

    • You can only see what's within vision range of your own queen and your own units — everything else is hidden, not just undiscovered. There's no persistent "explored" memory: moving a unit away hides whatever it revealed again.
    • Scouts see much farther than Workers or Soldiers, so sending one ahead is the best way to reveal distant parts of the map before committing other units there.
    • Hidden entities — other colonies' queens and workers, ruins, resource nodes, carcasses, aphids, and spiders — keep existing and moving while out of sight; they simply aren't sent to you until something of yours can see them again.
    • If your colony falls, you keep a small consolation view around your own ruin instead of a black screen.
    • Major world events (a nearby colony falling, a spider kill) can still appear in the event feed regardless of fog.

    Quick start

    1. Left-drag a box around your starting workers to select all of them.
    2. Click a nearby sugar, biomass, or protein node to send them gathering.
    3. Watch the totals at the top update as they carry resources home on their own.
    4. Once protein reaches 50, click "Worker" in the reproduce buttons to hatch a new one.
    5. Select some workers and click an aphid to hunt it, then gather the carcass it leaves behind.
    6. Pan around with WASD or a right-drag to explore — the world is shared with other colonies.
